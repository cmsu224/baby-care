    Milk & bottles

    The rule that stops the waste

    Warm only what you have, and let formula make up the rest. Breastmilk is the scarce thing; formula is unlimited and mixes fresh every time. So the order is breast → the small warmed portion → fresh formula up to target. Nothing precious is ever poured away.

    Store in 60 mL portions, not big ones. You can only waste what you thawed — so never thaw more than one feed's worth.

    Try him on fridge-cold or room-temperature milk. Warming is a preference, not a requirement. If he takes it cool, the whole problem disappears and 2 AM gets far easier. Worth one try at a daytime feed.

    How long it keeps

    • Freshly expressed, room temperature — 4 hours
    • Fridge — 4 days
    • Freezer — 6 months is best, 12 months acceptable
    • Thawed in the fridge — 24 hours. Never refreeze.
    • Warmed but not yet offered — use within 2 hours
    • Once he has drunk from the bottle — finish within 2 hours, then throw the rest away, however much is left. His mouth put bacteria in it.

    Warming

    • Bottle warmer, or stand the bottle in a mug of warm water for 5–10 minutes
    • Never a microwave. Hot spots scald, and it destroys the antibodies
    • Swirl, do not shake
    • Test on the inside of your wrist — it should feel like nothing at all
    • Separated layers are normal. Swirling brings the fat back in.

    Formula

    • Ready-to-feed is safest under 2 months — powder is not sterile
    • If using powder: water boiled and cooled to about 70°C / 158°F, then cooled quickly. Water first, then a level scoop.
    • Never dilute to stretch it, never concentrate it to add calories
    • Made up — 2 hours at room temperature, 24 hours in the fridge, 1 hour once he starts

    Red flags

    Emergency room now — do not call first

    • Rectal temperature 100.4°F / 38.0°C or higher. Give no fever medicine first.
    • Temperature below 97.7°F / 36.4°C that will not come up with skin-to-skin and a blanket
    • Trouble breathing: more than 60 breaths a minute at rest, grunting with each breath, nostrils flaring, or skin sucking in between or below the ribs
    • Pauses in breathing longer than 20 seconds, or any pause with colour change
    • Blue or dusky lips, tongue, or face. Blue hands and feet alone are normal.
    • Limp, floppy, or will not wake enough to feed
    • Seizure, or rhythmic jerking you cannot stop by holding the limb
    • Soft spot bulging when he is upright and calm, or clearly sunken
    • Green or yellow-green vomit, forceful projectile vomiting, or blood in vomit or stool
    • Any fall, drop, or blow to the head
    • A cry that is high-pitched, shrill, or weak and moaning rather than a normal angry cry

    Call the pediatrician today — same day, not tomorrow

    • Jaundice spreading below the belly button onto the legs or feet, or deepening in the whites of the eyes
    • Getting harder to wake for feeds, or feeding noticeably less than yesterday
    • Fewer than 6 wet diapers a day, or no wet diaper in 8 hours
    • No stool at all in 24 hours during these first weeks
    • Stool that turns white, chalky, or grey
    • Brick-red or orange powder in the diaper
    • Cord: spreading redness, pus, foul smell, or real bleeding
    • Straining to urinate, or dribbling rather than streaming
    • Inconsolable crying for hours that is unlike him
    • Not back to birth weight by day 14, or gaining under 20 g a day for two days running
    • Any rash with blisters or fluid-filled spots
    • Anyone with a cold sore has been near him
    • Your gut says something is wrong. That counts. Say exactly that on the phone.

    Have ready when you call

    Age in days and hours · birth weight and last known weight · rectal temperature · wet and dirty diapers in the last 24 hours · how much he has eaten and when he last fed · how far down the jaundice reaches.
